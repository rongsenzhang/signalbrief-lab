# From an alert to a customer update

A late backup generates a technical alert. A customer needs to know whether they can recover their data. Those questions need different evidence. This walkthrough uses one of Centerprise’s public service specifications to locate a realistic place for an NLP assistant between tool output, analyst assessment and the service desk. The examples and proposed interface belong to this prototype.

## Rehearsal with SB-103 and SB-105

1. In SB-103, E1031 establishes a missed backup window. E1032 says the delayed copy's restore verification has no result. The recovery team owns the follow-up. A service owner can be told that the check is pending and the records give no basis to conclude data loss.
2. Open SB-105 as a **separate case**. E1051 records another late backup. E1053 verifies a usable copy for its window. A customer update can narrow the uncertainty for that copy. The cause of the delay and the status of other copies remain outside this evidence.
3. Compare the experimental drafts. Both SB-105 outputs insert a scheduling error as the cause. A reviewer can use E1051 to correct them. In SB-103, “backup failed” would also wrongly turn a timing observation into a failed restore. [Recorded source review](draft-method.md#recorded-source-review)

A candidate integration could represent these decisions through an internal ticket with the affected service, source IDs, observed facts, known business impact, open question, next owner and review state. Sign-off records who checked which version before it enters the customer’s existing communications channel.

## Product boundaries

The UKRI grant names real-time threat detection and clear communication for readers with different technical backgrounds. The current recruitment pack refers to internal cybersecurity software. Those sources do not publish the product name, internal data schema, tenancy design, partner access or chosen model. The provider's naming of a vendor elsewhere cannot identify that product. Three public offerings establish different operating contexts. In a reported Defence Cyber Operations deployment Elastic supplied SIEM capabilities, Centerprise provided delivery, support and governance, and the Ministry's operators used the platform. CiContinuity’s managed detection service can use Microsoft Sentinel or SentinelOne according to customer requirements. Separately, Centerprise offers HPE-based private AI hosted on customer premises or in UK facilities. Together these justify a vendor-neutral event contract and a model that can run privately. They leave the KTP’s actual host, model and customer deployment to be established with the partner.
