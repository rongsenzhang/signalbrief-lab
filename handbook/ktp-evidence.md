# What the Essex–Centerprise KTP funds

Evidence reviewed on 22 November 2026. The public award gives the project two outcomes. The system should detect a potential threat as evidence arrives, and help people with different technical backgrounds decide how to respond. A useful demonstration therefore needs to show the moment evidence changes a decision, as well as the language used to explain it. ## Work packages and proposed acceptance tests

| Work package | Experiment | Acceptance evidence for a pilot decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Agree what counts as a detection | Select one decision with a service lead and an analyst. Have two reviewers label positive cases, look-alike benign cases, unknown outcomes and the earliest event sufficient to justify an alert. Keep an adjudication record. Split evaluation by case, time and source family. | The reviewers agree the adjudicated event and the safe disposition before testing. Record disagreements and label coverage. Keep future events unavailable to a detector assessing an earlier prefix. |
| 2. Detect changes in a stream | Replay interleaved subjects in event time. Include duplicated, missing, delayed and contrasting benign events. Compare a simple single-signal rule with corroborated evidence from independent sources. Measure alert precision, missed cases, false escalations per 1,000 benign sessions, and time from the adjudicated event to the first justified alert. Record end-to-end processing latency separately. | On the curated regression set, a duplicate cannot create independent confirmation, unrelated subjects cannot jointly trigger a finding and future evidence cannot alter an earlier output. For a partner trial, pre-agree acceptable missed-case and false-escalation rates and a p95 delay budget using the incumbent workflow. Report counts and uncertainty alongside percentages. |
| 3. Compare language tasks and costs | Give the same frozen case prefixes to a rules/template reference, a pinned private model and, when approved, a hosted model. Score classification on per-class precision, recall, macro-F1 and abstention coverage. Score simplified summaries against atomic facts, uncertainty and readability. Score instructions against a service-owner-approved checklist for responsible role, appropriate next check and escalation conditions. | Blind reviewers to provider and randomise output order. An unsupported material claim or an unjustified instruction sends a draft back to human review. Compare latency and total cost per accepted case, including review and correction time. Record model revision, prompt, inputs and outputs. Provider choice depends on these results and deployment approval. |
| 4. Prove the integration and a second workflow | Map one approved event feed to the adapter. Run fixture parity tests between the UI and API, exercise retries, and compare a reviewed security event with an operational recovery-verification ticket. Use synthetic or authorised redacted inputs and read-only access first. | Record field ownership, observed duplicates, rejected inputs and entitlement checks. The service owner can trace a displayed sentence and next step to permitted evidence in a given case. A recovery ticket carries its outstanding check to the next shift. |
| 5. Demonstrate adoption and value | Let two audience groups review matched, previously unseen cases with and without assistance. Counterbalance the order. Ask each reviewer to identify the known facts, unknown impact and next responsible person. Hand over a training exercise to another analyst and have them teach it back. | Report case-level time, material errors, comprehension, corrections and staff support cost. The service owner and academic supervisor can reproduce the comparison and decide whether to extend, revise or stop. Publish a method and an anonymised result only after the partner approves disclosure. |

## What to demonstrate first

1. Start with two similar sign-in streams. Show the earliest observation at which one justifies escalation, which observations support that decision and why the other case remains benign or unresolved. A replay can demonstrate event-time semantics. A claim about service latency requires a timed end-to-end test at representative arrival volumes.
2. Give an analyst the event evidence. Ask a service owner to explain what it means in their own words. For each audience, preserve uncertainty about identity and impact. Show which parts come from the detector, the language model and the human reviewer.
3. Present one counterexample alongside the successful case. An inaccurate fluent draft, a model abstention or a false escalation exposes a decision a deployment team must make. Show the baseline and the revision that would be tested next.
4. Take one independently verified observation into a handover or recovery ticket. An engineer should be able to map the public request and response to the screen and rerun the same check. Production fit depends on a partner-approved example and local access policy.
