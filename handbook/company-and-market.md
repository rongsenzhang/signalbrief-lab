# Centerprise and the market for assisted incident review

Research date **22 November 2026**. This independent application project uses public descriptions of how a UK managed service provider works. It uses fictional cases throughout.

## What the business sells

Centerprise International Limited is a privately held UK company incorporated in 1983 and based in Basingstoke. From its beginnings in PC engineering it has grown into an IT reseller, systems integrator and managed service provider. A customer can procure devices, host workloads, arrange operational support and recovery, and eventually dispose of assets through the group. Its markets include defence, government, health and emergency services, education, and commercial or regulated organisations. Public-sector procurement frameworks are one route to those customers. ## What to test in a pilot

| Hypothesis | How to test it with a service team |
| --- | --- |
| Analysts can validate evidence-linked summaries faster than they can reconstruct the same incident from raw records. | Counterbalance the order of manual and assisted review on matched, unseen cases. Measure review time and missed or unsupported facts. Record disagreements. |
| A service owner can act on plain-English handover without mistaking possible impact for an established outage. | Ask non-specialists to state the confirmed impact, uncertainty, owner and next action. Score comprehension against an agreed answer key. |
| Assisted handover can reduce repeated entry in a service desk. | Compare ticket completeness, time to hand over, correction rate and reopened tickets against the incumbent process. |
| A portable model interface can support customers with different hosting requirements. | Ask the partner to specify an approved data boundary, deployment target and latency budget. Compare quality, security review findings and cost per reviewed case before selecting a provider. |

The [Essex vacancy](https://vacancies.essex.ac.uk/tlive_webrecruitment/wrd/run/ETREC107GF.open?VACANCY_ID=208360f0kv&WVID=9918109NEm&LANG=USA) describes in-house cyber software and an intended UI/API integration. The material reviewed does not disclose that software's identity, event schema, architecture, authentication, approved model, customer data or interface contract. Those are discovery questions for the employer. The public demo's fixtures, schema and interfaces are independent examples. Production integration, human evaluation and commercial outcomes would require partner access and validation.
